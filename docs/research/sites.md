# Phase F dive-site field guide

**Scope.** These are the 13 catalog sites with terrain tiles. Dimensions are modelling targets where labelled **build**, not survey measurements. “Near” means the tile or a documented neighbouring field at comparable depth; an OBIS occurrence is evidence of presence, not abundance or precise placement. The local `data/landmarks/<id>/species.json` files retain the bounding boxes, depth filters and query URLs. Deep-site evidence is thin: do not turn regional or surface records into claimed wreck residents. All scene additions below are **game additions**, not mapped features. No downloadable assets are recommended in this document, so there are no asset licences to clear. Darkness begins below roughly 1,000 m; colours in these notes are what neutral white vehicle light reveals, while in-game ambient fill is an artistic readability choice. [NOAA light overview](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/light_travel.html), [OBIS occurrence caveats](https://obis.org/manual/access/).

**Life notation.** `★` marks a charismatic visual target. Sizes are approximate **model targets**, chosen from the observed organism's order of magnitude; they are not taxonomic size maxima. `Glow` means documented bioluminescence only; `unconfirmed` means do not make the organism glow. The linked field records support identities and depths; pigment and movement notes are visual interpretations unless an expedition image is linked. Microbes and small infauna belong in scan interactions rather than visible creature spawns.

## Lost City — 800 m

**Look.** This is an ultramafic massif with cream-to-white carbonate precipitate, pale tan talus, and dark serpentinized rock, not black-smoker basalt. The carbonate field has pinnacles, flanges, and delicate branches on a sloping terrace; Poseidon reaches about 60 m, so the highest skyline spans an entire local terrain cell. Diffuse alkaline fluid appears clear or milky rather than a black plume. At 800 m expect blue-black ambient light and long, white-light shadows; LEDs reveal chalky ivory, pale yellow and brown staining. [NOAA Lost City expedition](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/explorations/05lostcity/), [chimney study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016703706001840), [geologic map](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2015GC005869).

**Hero build.** A 55–60 m tall Poseidon cluster: 18 m wide buttressed base, several leaning cream carbonate columns, porous scalloped lips and 1–3 m overhanging flanges. Add small warm-white seep wisps and fine carbonate snow, with no orange lava or dark soot. Poseidon’s height is measured; the footprint and exact shape are reconstruction. [chimney study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016703706001840).

**Life.** The published field checklist supports tiny taxa rather than a dense macrofaunal spectacle: ★ wreckfish (_Polyprion americanus_, build 1 m, charcoal, slow cruiser, glow no); _Bouvierella curtirama_ amphipod (1–2 cm, translucent, hop/crawl, unconfirmed); sea anemones (Actiniaria spp., 5–15 cm, ivory/tan, sessile sway, no); gastropods (Gastropoda spp., 0.5–3 cm, pale, crawl, no); polychaete worms (Polychaeta spp., 1–5 cm, red/cream, crawl, unconfirmed); rare vent mussel (_Bathymodiolus azoricus_, 5–10 cm, brown shell, sessile, no); reef-building coral (_Desmophyllum pertusum_, 20–60 cm branch cluster, ivory, sessile, no). The mussel is extremely rare at the vent and the coral belongs on inactive carbonate a few metres away. Do **not** add giant tubeworms or swarming vent shrimp here. [NOAA Lost City macrofauna checklist](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/explorations/05lostcity/background/edu/media/lostcity05_dinner.pdf), [ecological study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3093485/), [MAR fauna review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2021.657358), [inactive-carbonate survey](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.898828/full).

**Game additions.** A low carbonate arch could form where adjacent flanges coalesce; a tight serpentine fissure is plausible on the fractured massif; a small abandoned carbonate stump can reveal old fluid pathways. **Event:** a temporary clear-flow pulse adds shimmering distortion and pale particles near a chimney; serpentinization-driven flow is the real mechanism. [fluid mixing study](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2023GC011011).

## Axial Seamount / ASHES — 1,530 m

**Look.** ASHES is a 60–80 m wide depression only a few metres deep on fractured lobate and sheet basalt, edged by rounded pillow lava. Black metal-sulfide chimneys are mostly under 4 m; warm diffuse fissures carry white bacterial film and red tube-worm bushes. This is fully aphotic. White light should separate charcoal pillows, rusty sulfide, scarlet worm plumes and white mats; the hot discharge itself can be surprisingly particle-poor. [OOI ASHES field description](https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/research-sites/axial-caldera/ashes/).

**Hero build.** Mushroom plus Inferno, separated by about 10 m: two rough, narrow 4 m stacks with many sub-5 cm spigots, friable spires and dense worm collars. Run a few clear jets from Mushroom and a darker sulfide plume from Inferno; keep plumes buoyant and bending with current. Use a 65 m shallow basin with fissures radiating from Mushroom. [OOI ASHES](https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/research-sites/axial-caldera/ashes/), [OOI Inferno](https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/inferno-vent-at-axial/).

**Life.** ★ Juan de Fuca tubeworm (_Ridgeia piscesae_, 0.2–1 m, white tube/red plume, sessile sway, no); vent limpet (_Lepetodrilus fucensis_, 1–2 cm, grey, crawl, no); vent snail (_Provanna variabilis_, 0.5–1 cm, tan, crawl, no); scale worm (_Cladopolynoe tunnicliffeae_, 2–5 cm, pale, crawl, unconfirmed); blob sculpin (_Psychrolutes phrictus_, 15–30 cm, mottled brown, bottom hover, no). These species have local depth-filtered OBIS records; OOI also visually documents tubeworms, limpets and scale worms at ASHES. [OBIS local extract](../../data/landmarks/axial-seamount-ashes/species.json), [OOI imagery](https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/research-sites/axial-caldera/ashes/).

**Game additions.** A fallen sulfide chimney with a newly venting crack is credible because these deposits are brittle; a pillow-lava tunnel follows lobate flow; a quiet bacterial-mat pocket lies beyond the main 80 m field. **Event:** a fissure warms and clouds briefly, then attracts crawling limpets over a longer timescale; volcanic fluid activity is monitored at Axial. [OOI ASHES](https://interactiveoceans.washington.edu/research-sites/axial-caldera/ashes/).

## Beebe / Piccard vent field — 4,960 m

**Look.** Beebe is basalt-hosted at about 4,950–4,980 m; dark broken rock and sulfide talus surround black smokers, with pale bacterial mats at diffuse-flow cracks. Terrain at 1–50 m scale should include a low gully, angular blocks and narrow vents rather than a smooth plain. No sunlight reaches it; LED light reveals near-black basalt, copper/iron rust, white anemones and translucent shrimp. Do not merge the shallower Von Damm white talc-like site into this scene. [discovery paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1636), [vent fauna study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3612092/).

**Hero build.** The documented sulfide mound is about 80 m across and 50 m high: build a dark stepped mass with a thick central black-smoker trunk, several 2–4 m subsidiary spires, black particulate rising above it, and a dense shrimp ring at cooler flow. Exact chimney heights and arrangement are design estimates; mound scale is sourced. [discovery paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms1636), [fauna study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3612092/), [Beebe thesis](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/362004/1/Nye_2C_20Verity_PhD.pdf).

**Life.** ★ Beebe vent shrimp (_Rimicaris hybisae_, 3–6 cm, translucent ivory, cluster/swim, no); Beebe vent snail (_Provanna beebei_, 0.5–1 cm, ivory-brown, crawl, no); Beebe anemone (_Maractis_ sp., 5–15 cm, white, sessile sway, no); squat lobster (Galatheoidea sp., 5–10 cm, white, crawl, no); bacterial mat (filamentous bacteria, microscopic, white film, sessile, no). Only the first two currently have confident species names from Beebe specimens; the other labels preserve the published identification level. [shrimp survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3612092/), [_Provanna_ description](https://academic.oup.com/mollus/article/85/4/425/5588462), [Beebe anemone survey](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/72496/noaa_72496_DS1.pdf), [Beebe expedition log](https://schmidtocean.org/wp-content/uploads/Appendix-One-Hydrothermal-Exploration-of-the-Mid-Cayman-Rise-Event-Logger-Report-FK008.pdf).

**Game additions.** A spent smoker stump is plausible beside active growth; an anemone-lined gully matches sampled Beebe habitat; a partly buried basalt fissure can vent a cooler trickle. **Event:** a stronger black plume briefly obscures a route, then current shears it aside. [shrimp survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3612092/).

## Challenger Deep — 10,925 m

**Look.** Treat the bottom as a quiet, soft, very fine, pale grey-brown sediment basin with shallow ripples, small slump scars and scattered organic fragments, not a volcanic chasm lined with glowing monsters. A lander or vehicle can easily lift a lingering silt cloud. There is no sunlight; under white LED the bottom is beige-grey, with particles making visibility poor near thrusters. The deeper trench walls can have steeper relief, but the floor itself is subdued. [NOAA depth](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/oceandepth.html), [sediment-core observations](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0967063709002192), [Mariana management plan](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2024-06/Mariana-Trench-Marine-National-Monument-Management-Plan-202405-FINAL.pdf).

**Hero build.** A 40 m **build** low-relief sediment hollow, with a 1–2 m slump edge and a solitary 5 cm amphipod at a dark wood fragment. The gasp is the immense empty horizon and depth readout; reserve the brightest cone for the tiny animal. These dimensions are scenic estimates, not mapped Challenger Deep structures. [_Hirondellea_ collection at 10,900 m](https://sioapps.ucsd.edu/collections/bi/catalog/C11848/?idx=29932).

**Life.** ★ giant hadal amphipod (_Hirondellea gigas_, 3–5 cm, ivory, hop/swim along bottom, no); agglutinated foraminifer (_Hormosina globulifera_, millimetres, sand-coloured test, immobile, no); _Lagenammina difflugiformis_ (millimetres, tan test, immobile, no); _Rhabdammina abyssorum_ (millimetres to centimetres, pale tube, immobile, no); _Leptohalysis kaikoi_ (millimetres, clear/pale, immobile, no). These named foraminifera come from Challenger Deep sediment samples, but are microscopic set dressing or scan finds. No fish is evidenced at this depth. [hadal amphipod paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3419748/), [foraminiferal record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0967063709002192).

**Game additions.** A small woodfall is plausible because _H. gigas_ can digest sunken plant matter; an unmarked soft-sediment slump is plausible on a trench floor; a compact microbial patch can mark buried organic matter. **Event:** an episodic detritus fall draws amphipods from a wide radius, followed by a persistent silt cloud if the vehicle touches down. [wood-digestion study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3419748/), [sediment microbiome study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6031389/).

## Kamaʻehuakanaloa — 975 m summit / deeper Pele's Pit

**Look.** The volcanic summit and Pele's Pit have black pillow basalt, fractured lava, orange-brown iron oxyhydroxide crusts and pale cream microbial veils around warm vents. Pit floor and rim are at different depths; avoid placing taxa recorded at 1,500–2,000 m on the 975 m summit without a depth transition. At 975 m ambient blue is extremely faint; white lamps reveal ochre, rust and yellow mats on dark rock. [USGS volcano account](https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/hvo/news/volcano-watch-kamaehuakanaloa-volcano-formerly-known-loihi-seamount), [iron-mat survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4393452/), [Nautilus pit video](https://nautiluslive.org/video/2018/08/31/surveying-peles-pit-crater-loihi-seamount).

**Hero build.** A 30 m **build** stepped crater rim and wall descending toward a warm iron-mat vent: black pillows, 2–5 m terraces, a 6 m ochre bacterial apron and translucent cream veil on the lip. The cliff and apron sizes are for the model; the iron-rich mats and pit setting are documented. [Nautilus pit video](https://nautiluslive.org/video/2018/08/31/surveying-peles-pit-crater-loihi-seamount), [iron-mat study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4393452/).

**Life.** ★ Hawaiʻi vent shrimp (_Opaepele loihi_, 2–4 cm, pale, dart/cluster, no); cutthroat eel (_Synaphobranchus brevidorsalis_, 30–60 cm, dark brown, slow cruiser, no); golden coral (_Chrysogorgia geniculata_, 0.5–1 m fan, gold under LED, sessile sway, no); spiral coral (_Iridogorgia bella_, 0.5–1 m, cream-gold, sessile sway, no); branching stony coral (_Enallopsammia rostrata_, 20–60 cm, white, sessile, no). The last three are from the tile's deeper 1,100–2,000 m slope records; place them below the summit, while the shrimp belongs at the vents. [USGS vent fauna](https://www.usgs.gov/news/volcano-watch-treasures-deep-sea-loihi-hosts-hydrothermal-vents), [OBIS local extract](../../data/landmarks/kamaehuakanaloa/species.json).

**Game additions.** A collapsed lava tube is plausible in pillow-rich basalt; a recently formed iron-oxide terrace can reveal active mat growth; a small fissure with a pale bacterial veil fits the documented vent chemistry. **Event:** a venting pulse briefly changes the mat's visible shimmer and kicks up orange particles. [iron-mat survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4393452/).

## Hunga Tonga–Hunga Ha'apai caldera — 850 m

**Look.** The post-2022 caldera reaches roughly 850 m and is about 4.8 km wide; its floor holds fresh grey ash and volcaniclastic sediment, and surrounding slopes show dramatic scours and bedforms from density flows. Deep vents were detected at 350–600 m, so a glowing smoker on the 850 m floor would be unsupported. Near the rim, blue ambient light and reef colour return; at the bottom, sunlight is negligible and LEDs reveal blue-grey ash, dark angular clasts and occasional pale deposit. [2026 repeat-bathymetry study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-026-02099-7), [density-flow study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43607-2).

**Hero build.** A 40 m **build** arc of jagged collapse wall above a 20 m high, curved sediment bedform. A descent through suspended ash exposes a deep quiet bowl. These local dimensions are scenic; documented bedforms elsewhere on the volcano reach about 20 m height. [density-flow study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43607-2).

**Life.** Evidence is shallow-rim biased; **none of the following is verified on the 850 m floor**. ★ blackfin chromis (_Pycnochromis vanderbilti_, 5–8 cm, dark blue, school, no); agile chromis (_Pycnochromis agilis_, 5–8 cm, blue-green, school, no); pearl chromis (_Pycnochromis margaritifer_, 5–8 cm, lavender-brown, school, no); white-tailed damselfish (_Pomacentrus vaiuli_, 5–10 cm, blue/white, dart, no); rainbow wrasse (_Thalassoma amblycephalum_, 10–20 cm, blue-green, dart, no). Use these only in shallow reef water; the 2022 survey saw no life on nearby fresh pyroclastic deposits, so keep the caldera floor nearly empty until post-eruption benthic observations are tied to species. [OBIS tile extract and depth fields](../../data/landmarks/hunga-tonga-caldera/species.json), [post-eruption survey](https://en.nippon-foundation.or.jp/news/articles/2022/20220525-71696.html).

**Game additions.** A loose ash bank fits the fresh deposits; a half-buried basalt dike is plausible on a volcanic wall; a narrow collapse alcove is plausible along the caldera margin. **Event:** a modest sediment slump sends a lateral turbidity cloud across the path. Keep it local rather than recreating the catastrophic 2022 eruption. [density-flow study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43607-2).

## Monterey Canyon — upper canyon to 3,600 m fan

**Look.** A sand-and-mud axis with sharp sediment boundaries runs between steep layered canyon walls, ledges and rock outcrops; at 3,000 m the channel grades into the deep fan. At 1–50 m scale, use ripple fields, fresh flow scours, collapsed wall blocks and localized muddy banks. The deep floor has no ambient sunlight, and white light reveals olive-tan mud, grey sand and pale organisms. A recent flow should reduce visibility from clear water to brown suspension, then settle. [MBARI canyon overview](https://www.mbari.org/know-your-ocean/monterey-canyon/), [MBARI core survey](https://www.mbari.org/news/following-the-trail-of-sand-in-monterey-canyon/), [flow observations](https://www.mbari.org/news/high-tech-smart-boulders-give-the-first-look-from-inside-an-underwater-landslide/).

**Hero build.** A 35 m **build** undercut bend: 18 m high layered wall, 6 m fresh slump scar, tilted boulders and a tight sandy thalweg. Lights catch a sudden veil of moving silt along the floor. The local shape is invented; MBARI's observed sediment flows can mobilize the upper metres of the seafloor. [MBARI flow observations](https://www.mbari.org/news/high-tech-smart-boulders-give-the-first-look-from-inside-an-underwater-landslide/).

**Life.** The tile OBIS records below are mostly 200–2,100 m, so stage them along the descent, **not** on the 3,600 m fan: ★ chain siphonophore (_Nanomia bijuga_, 10–30 cm colony, clear, drifting chain, glow unconfirmed); ★ midwater worm (_Poeobius meseres_, 2–4 cm, translucent, drift, glow unconfirmed); sea pen (_Umbellula lindahli_, 0.5–1.5 m, pink/cream, sessile sway, no); California sun star (_Rathbunaster californicus_, 20–50 cm, tan, crawl, no); market squid prey fish / Pacific hake (_Merluccius productus_, 30–60 cm, silver, school, no). At the deepest mission depth, use a sparse unidentified demersal silhouette until a depth-matched voucher is added. [OBIS depth-filtered tile extract](../../data/landmarks/monterey-canyon/species.json), [MBARI siphonophore](https://www.mbari.org/animal/common-siphonophore/), [MBARI balloon worm](https://www.mbari.org/animal/balloon-worm/).

**Game additions.** An abandoned instrument frame is plausible because MBARI has deployed detectors here; a buried whale vertebra is plausible where organic material gathers in a canyon; a small ledge cave can form under a resistant bed. **Event:** a localized turbidity current sweeps down-channel with a denser basal head and trailing cloud, then exposes new objects. [MBARI experiment](https://annualreport.mbari.org/2017/story/coordinated-canyon-experiment).

## Hudson Canyon — upper canyon to 3,000 m

**Look.** This canyon has steep slopes, firm outcrops and multiple sediment types, with dark muddy lower channels and rocky ledges. At local scale use metre-sized step faults, slump slabs and a channel bed of silt, sand and shell fragments. The upper canyon can be green-blue and hazy; the 3,000 m floor is fully dark under the vehicle LEDs. NOAA specifically says the deep coral habitat remains under-surveyed, so avoid an uninterrupted coral forest across the lower tile. [NOAA sanctuary overview](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/hudson-canyon/), [NOAA coral portal](https://www.deepseacoraldata.noaa.gov/regions/northeast).

**Hero build.** A 45 m **build** canyon junction: two high layered walls and a single orange-pink coral garden on a hard ledge 10–20 m above the mud axis. Sonar reveals the narrow cross-cut before headlights do. The coral patch is a plausible local addition, not a mapped Hudson colony. [NOAA canyon description](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/hudson-canyon/), [2025 ROV expedition](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/news/2025/explore-the-depths-hudson-canyon-live.html).

**Life.** These are documented in the canyon region but mostly at **upper-water or slope depths**, not proven at the 3,000 m floor: ★ sperm whale (_Physeter macrocephalus_, 10–18 m, dark slate, large cruiser above canyon, no); deepwater octopus (_Graneledone verrucosa_, 20–50 cm, brick red, cephalopod crawl/hover, no); blue shark (_Prionace glauca_, 1.5–3 m, blue, large cruiser in upper water, no); silver hake (_Merluccius bilinearis_, 30–60 cm, silver, school on slope, no); deep coral (_Paramuricea_ sp., 0.3–1 m, orange/pink, sessile sway on hard ledge, no). Whale, octopus and coral are supported by NOAA accounts; shark and hake have tile OBIS records with shallow depth limits. [NOAA sanctuary](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/hudson-canyon/), [NOAA expedition](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/news/2025/explore-the-depths-hudson-canyon-live.html), [OBIS tile extract](../../data/landmarks/hudson-canyon/species.json).

**Game additions.** A snagged old fishing net is plausible in a fished canyon; a small mudslide scar follows active slope erosion; a whale bone on a lower ledge is plausible because whales use the canyon. **Event:** a passing sperm whale is a distant moving shadow and sound from the upper water, while a smaller sediment pulse can occur deep below. [NOAA sanctuary](https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/hudson-canyon/).

## Blake Plateau coral mounds — 750 m

**Look.** The 500–1,000 m plateau carries cold-water coral mounds of dead white coral rubble and live branching colonies, separated by pale carbonate sand and hard ledges. Small mound crests and saddles provide metre-to-tens-of-metres relief that a coarse tile smooths away. There is little natural seabed light at 750 m; white LEDs reveal bright ivory live coral, tan skeleton, orange and lavender octocorals, and brown sediment. [NOAA mapping news](https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/news/million-mounds-news/), [mound geomorphology](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/64470/noaa_64470_DS1.pdf), [NOAA coral review](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/481/noaa_481_DS1.pdf).

**Hero build.** A 25 m **build** reef mound with 8 m local relief: a pale talus skirt, 3–5 m stepped dead-coral core and an interlocking crown of white branches. Put a dark channel between adjacent mounds so the crest emerges all at once. The exact single mound shape is reconstruction; the mound province and taxa are documented. [NOAA mapping paper](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/64470/noaa_64470_DS1.pdf).

**Life.** ★ reef-building cold-water coral (_Desmophyllum pertusum_, 0.3–2 m colony, ivory, sessile sway, no); ★ deep-water coral (_Enallopsammia profunda_, 0.3–1 m colony, cream, sessile sway, no); eye coral (_Madrepora oculata_, 0.2–0.8 m, white, sessile sway, no); stylaster lace coral (_Stylaster erubescens_, 0.1–0.3 m, rose, sessile, no); little sea urchin (_Echinocyamus grandiporus_, 1–3 cm, sand-coloured, crawl, no). All five have 500–1,000 m tile OBIS records. [OBIS depth-filtered tile extract](../../data/landmarks/blake-plateau-corals/species.json), [NOAA regional coral review](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/481/noaa_481_DS1.pdf).

**Game additions.** A coral-rubble arch is plausible where old skeleton breaks; an isolated hardground patch can hold a colourful fan; a buried sponge garden can occupy a low-current lee. **Event:** a current pulse bends living branches and sends a bright marine-snow stream across the mound. [NOAA mapping paper](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/64470/noaa_64470_DS1.pdf).

## Great Blue Hole — 0–124 m

**Look.** The 320 m wide flooded sinkhole has a bright reef rim, nearly vertical limestone walls, submerged stalactites around the diveable upper interior, and a soft sediment floor. Below a hydrogen-sulfide/low-oxygen layer near 90 m, keep the scene empty of reef fish; the contrast between turquoise rim and dim cobalt shaft is the key visual. LEDs reveal cream limestone and pale stalactites above, then grey sediment below. The 124–125 m depth and 320 m width have survey support; anoxic chemistry varies by layer and time. [2025 sediment-core study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dep2.70021), [expedition survey account](https://www.gpsworld.com/belizes-great-blue-hole-revealed-in-expedition-survey/), [2013 seismic study](https://limnogeology.ethz.ch/GischlerMarine.pdf).

**Hero build.** A 35 m **build** traverse of the 40 m-depth stalactite gallery: massive pendant limestone columns, a shadowed undercut and a clear-to-milky chemocline below. Stalactite spacing and individual sizes are artistic; their presence and drowned cave origin are documented. [sediment-core study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dep2.70021).

**Life.** Rim/upper-wall only: ★ Caribbean reef shark (_Carcharhinus perezi_, 1.5–2.5 m, grey, large cruiser, no); blue tang (_Acanthurus coeruleus_, 15–25 cm, blue, school/graze, no); French grunt (_Haemulon flavolineatum_, 15–25 cm, yellow-striped silver, school, no); stoplight parrotfish (_Sparisoma viride_, 25–50 cm, green-blue, school/graze, no); ★ symmetrical brain coral (_Pseudodiploria strigosa_, 0.3–1 m dome, tan-brown, sessile, no). The OBIS depths for these tile species are mostly 3–11 m. Do not carry them through the low-oxygen lower shaft. [OBIS rim-depth extract](../../data/landmarks/great-blue-hole/species.json), [Lighthouse Reef context](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/764/).

**Game additions.** A small side recess is plausible in karst limestone; a fallen stalactite block fits a drowned cave; a buried shell layer can record old sea-level change. **Event:** a passing shark crosses the bright opening, while a transient particle veil marks the chemocline. [sediment-core study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/dep2.70021).

## RMS Titanic — 3,800 m

**Look.** The largely upright bow section is about 140 m long and points roughly north; the shattered stern lies roughly 600–700 m south. Flat clay-like tan-grey sediment and isolated glacial stones make the wreck read sharply. The dense debris trails run southwest, and boilers, hull plates, crockery and fittings punctuate the dark plain. No ambient sunlight: LEDs reveal reddish rusticles, orange-brown steel, pale sediment and occasional white/pink invertebrates. Rusticles hang in irregular curtains rather than uniform rust texture. [WHOI site map](https://www.whoi.edu/oceanus/feature/the-quest-to-map-titanic/), [WHOI first-dive footage](https://www.whoi.edu/press-room/news-release/woods-hole-oceanographic-institution-releases-rare-video-footage-from-the-first-submersible-dives-to-rms-titanic/), [WHOI 2010 survey](https://www.whoi.edu/press-room/news-release/whoi-team-uses-advanced-imaging-data-to-bring-a-new-view-of-titanic-to-the-world/).

**Hero build.** The bow emerging prow-first: 140 m long, 28 m full-beam reference, 12–18 m visible height **build** above sediment; sharp prow, anchor openings/chains, two capstans, collapsed deck edge and layered rusticle curtains. Keep a separate chaotic stern far enough away to require a journey. The bow silhouette, chains and capstans are documented; visible height is a reconstruction. ## Bismarck — 4,791 m

**Look.** The battleship rests largely upright on an abyssal slope; the stern is broken away and major gun turrets are displaced. It is a dark steel mass with ragged grey-brown sediment around its impact path, not a pristine floating ship. At 1–50 m scale build a scrape, small slide blocks and hull-fragment islands that coarse terrain misses. No sunlight; white lamps pick out dark steel, tan silt, reddish corrosion and isolated pale deposit feeders. [wreck survey summary](https://www.kbismarck.com/wreck.html), [Magellan ROV survey](https://www.magellan.gg/vrov-pilot-bismarck/), [nearby 4,850 m Porcupine Abyssal Plain research](https://www.noc.ac.uk/our-work/ships-and-expeditions/expeditions/jc278).

**Hero build.** A 250 m **build** readable upright hull with 36 m beam reference, missing stern, torn aft edge, deck gun rings and two enormous detached turret masses in the sediment. Silhouette and breakage should dominate before small details. Exact part placement remains survey-dependent; the build size approximates the historical ship. [wreck survey](https://www.kbismarck.com/wreck.html), [Magellan survey](https://www.magellan.gg/vrov-pilot-bismarck/).

**Life.** No verified species-level census of the wreck floor was found. The Porcupine Abyssal Plain observatory about the same depth and in the nearby northeast Atlantic supports a conservative **regional** set: ★ purple long-tailed sea cucumber (_Psychropotes buglossa_, 15–30 cm, purple, crawl, no); _Oneirophanta mutabilis_ sea cucumber (10–25 cm, pale, crawl, no); _Pseudostichopus villosus_ sea cucumber (10–30 cm, tan, crawl, no); _Amperima rosea_ sea cucumber (5–15 cm, pink, crawl, no); _Deima validum_ sea cucumber (10–20 cm, pale, crawl, no). Do not claim these individuals were photographed on Bismarck. [PAP holothurian study](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/28690/), [PAP population change](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0967064509000186), [PAP _Psychropotes_ voucher and reidentification](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11336395/), [PAP cruise species list](https://www.bodc.ac.uk/resources/inventories/cruise_inventory/reports/d255.pdf).

**Game additions.** A small detached boat fragment is plausible along a wreck debris trail; a low sediment slump follows the impact scar; an isolated dropped chain can anchor a sonar secret. **Event:** a seasonal marine-snow pulse brings a temporary increase in slow deposit-feeder activity, grounded in observed PAP food-supply responses. ## Endurance — 3,008 m

**Look.** The 44 m wooden polar vessel is upright, tall above a flat, fine-sediment Weddell Sea floor; stern lettering and a pole-star emblem remain visible. Cold water and the absence of destructive wood-borers helped preservation. At 1–50 m scale the hull, short spars and scattered debris are the relief; the plain should remain quiet. No sunlight arrives, and white lamps show dark honey-brown timber, pale sediment, white anemones and occasional pink starfish. The wreck is a protected historic site: interactions should be observation-only. [Endurance22 discovery](https://endurance22.org/endurance-is-found), [heritage protection](https://ukaht.org/places/endurance-shipwreck/), [wreck fauna observations](https://fmht.co.uk/shackletons-endurance-revealed/endurance-at-the-bottom-of-the-sea/).

**Hero build.** A 44 m upright timber hull, 8 m **build** beam, 8–10 m **build** visible relief; stern name in an arch, pole-star emblem, intact rail contours, short broken masts and light encrusting life. Give it a broad dark-water reveal from above, leaving the structure untouched. Length, upright condition and name are sourced; beam and visible relief are build estimates. [Endurance22 discovery](https://endurance22.org/endurance-is-found).

**Life.** Wreck imagery supports forms but not confident species names at 3,008 m. Keep labels at observed rank: ★ white sea anemone (Actiniaria sp., 5–15 cm, white, sessile sway, no); brisingid sea star (Brisingida sp., 10–30 cm span, pale, crawl/suspend arms, no); probable squat lobster (_Munidopsis_ sp., 5–10 cm, ghost white, crawl, no); probable glass sponge (Hexactinellida sp., 10–40 cm, white, sessile, no); sea squirt (Ascidiacea sp., 2–10 cm, translucent, sessile, no); ★ sea lily (Crinoidea sp., 10–30 cm crown, yellow, sessile sway, no). The squat-lobster and glass-sponge ranks are provisional image identifications; do not turn nearby shallow OBIS penguin and seal records into bottom fauna. [analysis of Endurance22 images](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/shackletons-endurance-shipwreck-is-teeming-with-bizarre-ocean-life-180979719/), [local OBIS extract limitations](../../data/landmarks/endurance/species.json).

**Game additions.** A shallow iceberg-dropstone cluster is plausible on the polar floor; a small drift of marine snow against the keel is plausible; a detached rigging bundle could lie nearby but should be labelled reconstruction. **Event:** a brief dense marine-snow fall passes through the beam, with a faint distant ice-fracture sound from the surface system. [Endurance22 oceanography](https://endurance22.org/science/meteorology-oceanography).

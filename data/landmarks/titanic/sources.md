## Coordinate notes and discrepancies

- **Bow and stern.** Sources 2 and 3 give the same arc-second coordinates (bow 41°43′57″N 49°56′49″W = 41.73250, -49.94694; stern 41°43′35″N 49°56′54″W = 41.72639, -49.94833). The bow value also matches `data/landmarks.json`. These are rounded to 1″ (~31 m N–S, ~23 m E–W), and it is not stated whether they mark the centre or one end of each section.
- **Separation.** The published coordinates are **690 m** apart (bearing 190° bow→stern). Text sources say ~600 m / 1,970–2,000 ft. The ~90 m difference is within the coordinate rounding plus the centre-versus-end ambiguity. We keep the published coordinates.
- **Boiler field.** The Wikipedia infobox coordinate 41°43′32″N 49°56′49″W (41.72556, -49.94694) is footnoted as the centre of the boiler field, citing Ballard 1987 p. 199, and titanicstory.com gives the same coordinate for the boilers. The POI and prop use it (confidence medium). From the published stern coordinate it lies 148 m at bearing 126° (south-east). Wikipedia's text says "about 600 ft (180 m) east of the stern", which is consistent within the coordinate rounding. _Correction:_ an earlier draft paraphrased secondary sources as "between the sections" and placed the boilers 200 m north of the stern. That was wrong and has been fixed.
- **Headings.** No numeric heading is published in any source consulted. Bow: "faces north" (source 3) → `heading_deg` 0 (medium). Stern: "rear points to the bow section" (source 5) → rear end ~10° (the stern→bow bearing is 9.6°), so the ship's forward end is 190°. The hull blocks are now directional (`ends` in `props.json`, see docs/props.md): the bow is `[prow, cut]`, so its prow points north and its torn end faces the stern. The stern is `[cut, rounded]`, so its torn forward end faces 190° and its rounded counter stern points back at the bow.
- **Depth.** Published figures vary: 3,800 m (Wikipedia), "more than 12,400 ft" = >3,780 m (WHOI 1985 page), "12,600 feet" = 3,840 m (Oceanus 2012). The 3,784 m figure in the brief was not found in any source consulted and is not used.
- **Death toll.** Figures vary: 1,523 (the Agreement), 1,513 (UPI 1986), "approximately 1,500" (Wikipedia). The text uses "about 1,500".
- **Pressure.** Wikipedia says "over 6,000 psi". Computed ρgh (ρ = 1,027 kg/m³, 3,800 m) + 1 atm ≈ 379 atm ≈ 5,570 psi, so the briefing uses "about 380 atmospheres".
- **Positions not published** (as coordinates): debris-field centre, deck fittings, Big Piece site, memorial-plaque spot. All are placed by description and flagged `confidence: "low"` (plaque: `"medium"`, since its location on the stern is well described). The Big Piece POI sits ~175 m south-west of the stern, along the south-west debris trail (Ballard 1987, p. 207). It was moved there from east of the stern to avoid overlapping the boiler field.

## Verification log: QA-B #9 and #16 (2026-09-22)

Pages re-fetched (Wikipedia as raw wikitext, the Oceanus PDF as text) to check three claims flagged by Phase B QA.

- **Debris-field extent (#9).** The old text said "roughly 3 by 5 km (about 15 square miles)". That mixes units: 3 x 5 km is 15 km² (~5.8 sq mi), while 15 sq mi is ~39 km². No source consulted says "3 by 5 km". Wikipedia's "3 x 5" is in nautical miles (source 1, citing AP 2012), and neither Oceanus (source 5) nor The History Blog (source 7) gives linear dimensions. The text now separates two things. (1) The **two dense debris fields near the sections**: each 600–800 m long, about 5 km² / 2 sq mi together (source 1, "Debris fields", citing Ballard 1987 p. 207 and Rubin 1987). (2) The **full scatter mapped in 2010**: 15–20 sq mi, about 39–52 km² (source 5; source 7 says 15 sq mi). Changed in `guide.json` (overview and debris-field entry, including its facts), `pois.json` (debris-field note) and `props.json` (debris-midfield note).
- **"Two main debris trails run south-west" (#16b).** Confirmed. Source 1, section "Debris fields", says the two fields trail "in a southwesterly direction from the bow and stern", citing Ballard 1987, p. 207. Sources 5 and 7 do not give a direction. The citations in `pois.json`, `props.json` and this file now name the section.
- **Big Piece "C and D decks" (#16a).** The Big Piece article (source 16) does not name the decks. The "Wreck of the Titanic" article (source 1, section "1986–1998") does ("two first-class cabins on C Deck, extending down to D Deck"), but the sentence is uncited. RMS Titanic, Inc. (source 23) independently says "portholes from C-Deck cabins to D-Deck kitchens". The claim is kept, reworded to "part of the wall of first-class cabins on C Deck, extending down to D Deck", and source 23 is added to the entry's sources. Size figures still differ between articles (source 16: 20 x 26 ft, 15 short tons now, ~20 short tons when raised; source 1: 15 x 25 ft, 20 tons for the 1996 attempt). The guide keeps 7.9 x 6.1 m and 15–20 tons.

## Not verified / open

- No numeric headings for either section were found in any source consulted. A georeferenced 2010 sonar mosaic (RMST/WHOI) would settle both headings and the debris-item positions, but it is not openly published.
- The 1986 Explorers Club plaque on a bow capstan comes from a single source (Paul Lee, citing Ballard's autobiography).
- Individual bollards and deck cranes in the debris field were not confirmed in any source consulted. D3 removed the unmarked deck-fittings POI and its guide entry; the mapped seabed channel remains a terrain scan.
- The Magellan 2022 scan (source 21) would give exact geometry but is proprietary.
